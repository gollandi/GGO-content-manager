# PIF Tick Requirements for Social Content

These requirements apply **only when JJ explicitly activates PIF Tick mode** for a batch. They are in addition to the GMC guardrails (which always apply).

PIF Tick (Patient Information Forum Tick) is a UK quality mark for health information. Applying it to social media is non-standard — PIF Tick was designed for longer-form patient information leaflets and web content. This reference adapts the core principles to the social media format.

## Core PIF Tick Principles Applied to Social

### 1. Evidence-Based Content
Every clinical claim must cite its source. For social media, this means:

**In the caption:**
- Brief inline reference: "According to NICE guidelines..." or "EAU 2025 recommends..."
- Or a source line at the end: "📚 Source: NICE CG97 / EAU Guidelines on Male Sexual Dysfunction 2025"

**What counts as a source:**
- NICE guidelines (cite the guideline number)
- EAU/AUA guidelines (cite the year)
- Peer-reviewed publications (cite first author + journal + year)
- NHS.uk content (cite the page topic)

**What does NOT count:**
- "Studies show..." without specifying which studies
- Wikipedia or patient forums
- News articles without underlying research

### 2. Accessible Language
Social media is inherently accessible in format (short, visual), but captions must still meet readability standards.

- **Target reading age**: 12-14 (Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level 7-9)
- **Medical jargon**: If a medical term is used, follow it with a parenthetical explanation on first use
  - ✅ "Varicocele (enlarged veins in the scrotum)"
  - ✅ "Peyronie's disease — a condition where scar tissue causes the penis to curve"
  - ❌ "Hypogonadotropic hypogonadism" without explanation
- **Sentence length**: Average 15-20 words per sentence. Break up long sentences.
- **Active voice**: Prefer "The surgeon performs the procedure" over "The procedure is performed by the surgeon"

### 3. Author Attribution
Every PIF Tick caption must include clear authorship.

**Format options (pick one per post):**
- "Written by Mr Giangiacomo Ollandini FRCS(Eng), Consultant Urological Surgeon & Andrologist"
- "Content by Mr G. Ollandini, ggomed.co.uk"
- Bio reference: "See @ggomed.co.uk for author credentials" (only if the bio is up to date)

### 4. Review Date
PIF Tick requires content to have a review date. For social media:

- Add at the end of the caption: "Content reviewed: [Month Year]"
- Use the current month/year at time of batch production
- This signals to patients that the information is current

### 5. Patient-Centred Framing
Content should be framed around patient needs, not practitioner promotion.

**PIF Tick framing:**
- ✅ "Understanding your options for treating erectile dysfunction"
- ✅ "What to expect during a varicocele assessment"
- ❌ "Why you should choose Mr Ollandini for your varicocele surgery"
- ❌ "Book your consultation today — limited availability"

In PIF Tick mode, CTAs should be informational ("Learn more at ggomed.co.uk") rather than commercial ("Book now").

### 6. Inclusivity
- Use gender-inclusive language where clinically appropriate
- "People with testes" or "men and others with male reproductive anatomy" when relevant
- Don't over-correct where it obscures meaning — "men's health" is fine as a category
- Consider diverse patient populations in examples and framing

## PIF Tick Caption Template

```
[Hook line — patient-centred question or insight]

[Educational content — 2-4 sentences, accessible language, jargon explained]

[Clinical claim with source reference]

Written by Mr Giangiacomo Ollandini FRCS(Eng)
Content reviewed: [Month Year]
📚 [Source citation]

[Hashtags]
```

## Example

```
What happens during a penile Doppler ultrasound? 🔬

This painless scan uses sound waves to measure blood flow in the penis. It helps identify whether erectile dysfunction has a vascular cause — meaning the blood vessels aren't working as they should.

The European Association of Urology recommends vascular assessment as part of a comprehensive ED workup (EAU Guidelines 2025).

Written by Mr Giangiacomo Ollandini FRCS(Eng)
Content reviewed: April 2026
📚 Source: EAU Guidelines on Male Sexual Dysfunction, 2025

#ErectileDysfunction #PenileDoppler #MensHealth #Urology #PatientEducation #GGOMed
```
