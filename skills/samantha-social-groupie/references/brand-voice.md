# GGOMed Social Media Brand Voice

## Who Is Speaking

Mr Giangiacomo Ollandini — a Consultant Urological Surgeon & Andrologist in private practice in London. Italian-born, FRCS(Eng), based at Chelsea & Westminster Hospital Private Care and Nuffield Health Highgate. He explains complex urology in plain language without dumbing it down. He's not a "content creator" — he's a clinician who educates.

## Voice Characteristics

**Authoritative but approachable.** JJ knows his field inside out and isn't afraid to be direct, but he never talks down to patients. Think: the specialist who sits with you after clinic and explains things properly.

**First person for opinion/experience.** When the caption reflects JJ's clinical perspective:
- "In my practice, I see this pattern regularly."
- "I always tell my patients..."
- "This is one of the most misunderstood conditions I treat."

**Third person for educational facts.** When the caption is purely informational:
- "Erectile dysfunction affects approximately 1 in 5 men over 40."
- "The European Association of Urology recommends..."

**Never:**
- Corporate buzzwords ("holistic journey", "empowering wellness", "cutting-edge solutions")
- Clickbait hyperbole ("SHOCKING truth about...", "Doctors DON'T want you to know...")
- American spellings or terminology
- "Dr Ollandini" — always "Mr Ollandini" (surgeons in the UK use Mr)
- Informal abbreviations in professional context ("gonna", "wanna", "ur")

## Tone Calibration by Platform

| Platform | Register | Length | Emoji |
|----------|----------|--------|-------|
| Instagram | Warm, educational, slightly conversational | 150-300 words | Sparingly — 1-3 per caption, never in clinical claims |
| LinkedIn | Professional, reflective, may include personal clinical insight | Up to 500 words | Rarely or never |
| YouTube (descriptions) | Informative, structured | Variable | Minimal |
| Facebook | Similar to Instagram but slightly more conversational | 100-250 words | Light use OK |

## Hook Lines

The first line of every caption must earn the scroll-stop. Patterns that work for GGOMed:

- **Myth challenge**: "Most men think erectile dysfunction is just about blood flow. It's not."
- **Stat opener**: "40% of men with ED have an underlying cardiovascular condition."
- **Direct address**: "If you've been told 'it's just stress', read this."
- **Clinical insight**: "This is the question I get asked most in clinic."
- **Contrarian**: "Testosterone replacement isn't always the answer."

Patterns to avoid:
- Questions that feel formulaic ("Did you know that...?")
- All-caps shock ("YOU WON'T BELIEVE...")
- Vague teasers ("The truth about men's health...")

## Vocabulary

| Use | Don't use |
|-----|-----------|
| erectile dysfunction | impotence, ED (alone as hashtag) |
| fertility | virility |
| urological surgeon | urologist (acceptable but less precise) |
| assessment, investigation | check-up, MOT |
| treatment options | solutions, fixes |
| evidence suggests | science proves, studies show |
| may improve | will fix, guarantees |
| book a consultation | book now, act fast |

## CTA Rotation

Don't use the same call-to-action for consecutive posts. Rotate through:
1. "Save this for later ↓"
2. "Link in bio for the full guide"
3. "Questions? Drop them in the comments"
4. "Share this with someone who might need it"
5. "Book a consultation — link in bio"
6. "More on this topic at ggomed.co.uk"
7. [No CTA] — sometimes the content stands on its own

## Emoji Usage

- Clinical claims: NO emoji
- CTA lines: One directional emoji OK (↓, →)
- Section breaks in longer captions: A single • or — is preferred over emoji
- Topic markers: Acceptable sparingly (🔬 for research, ❓ for myth-busting)
- Never: 🍆, 💪, 🔥, or anything that undermines clinical authority
