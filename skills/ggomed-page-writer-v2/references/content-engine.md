## The Voice Formula: Warm + Blunt + Precise

Think: competent friend who happens to be a consultant urologist.

### 1. Emotionally validating without being saccharine
- Acknowledge feelings: "It's very common to worry about cancer when you see blood"
- Then orient: "Let me explain what we usually find"
- No fake reassurance: "Most causes aren't cancer" NOT "Don't worry, it's nothing!"

### 2. Frank about bodies and sex
- Use anatomically correct terms: penis, testicles, erections, ejaculation
- Also use plain language: having sex, getting hard, coming too quickly
- NO euphemisms: "intimacy issues" when you mean erectile dysfunction
- NO pornographic language
- Normalise: "Erections involve complex coordination of nerves, blood vessels, and psychology"

### 3. Honest about evidence and uncertainty
- Strong: "Studies consistently show…"
- Moderate: "Research suggests…" / "Many specialists believe…"
- Weak: "The evidence here is limited" / "Opinion is divided"
- Personal: "In my practice, I've found…" / "My approach is…"
- Unknown: "We don't fully understand why this happens"

### 4. Precision without jargon
- Technical term + immediate explanation: "varicocele — enlarged veins in the scrotum,
  like varicose veins"
- Analogies for complex processes
- Mechanisms explained simply

### 5. Structured but conversational
- Clear headings for scanning
- Logical flow: problem → explanation → investigation → options → next steps
- No marketing fluff, no hype, no clickbait

### 6. Non-judgemental and inclusive
- No assumptions about sexuality, gender identity, relationship status
- "Your partner" not "your wife/girlfriend"
- "People with penises" when trans-inclusive language appropriate
- No body-shaming, no STI stigma

### 7. Marketing tone: subtle and trustworthy
GOOD: "In my clinic, I typically start with…" / "At GGO Med, we follow BAUS guidelines…"
BAD: "Our world-class team of experts…" / "Revolutionary treatment…" / "Guaranteed results"
Frame around WHAT you do and HOW you do it, not superlatives.

## Language Rules

Always:
- UK English (colour, recognise, labour, programme, centre, haematuria)
- Second person "you" for reader
- First person "I" for JJ
- Present tense where possible
- Active voice
- Short paragraphs (2–4 sentences)
- Year 11 reading level (accessible, not patronising)

Never:
- US spellings (tumor, center, analyze, behavior)
- Corporate "we" (unless genuinely collaborative)
- Passive constructions that obscure responsibility
- Walls of text

## Concrete Voice Examples

### Haematuria intro (model for emotional entry)
```
Seeing blood in your urine is frightening. Your mind probably went straight to cancer —
that's a completely normal reaction, and it's exactly why you should get it checked properly.

Here's what I need you to know: most haematuria (the medical term for blood in urine)
isn't caused by cancer. But some cases are, and we can't tell which without proper
investigation. That's why we take every case seriously.

Let me walk you through what haematuria actually means, what causes it, and how we
investigate it thoroughly.
```

### SSRIs for PE (model for mechanism explanation + honest effectiveness)
```
SSRIs — originally antidepressant medications — have a well-known "side effect" of
delaying ejaculation. We use this effect therapeutically for PE.

Dapoxetine (Priligy®) is the only SSRI licensed specifically for PE in the UK. You take
it 1–3 hours before sex. The evidence shows SSRIs can increase ejaculatory time by around
2–8 times baseline, though this varies considerably between individuals. It's not a magic
fix, but for many men it makes sex more satisfying.
```

### TRT + PSA counselling (model for shared decision-making)
```
If your PSA is elevated or rising, we need to understand why before starting or
continuing TRT. An elevated PSA doesn't automatically mean cancer, but testosterone can
stimulate prostate cancer growth if it's present. So we investigate first.

This isn't me being overly cautious — it's standard urological practice. The decision
is yours, but I'll be clear about what I think makes sense in your situation.
```

## Voice Anti-Patterns

❌ Corporate medical speak:
"Our multidisciplinary team utilises cutting-edge diagnostic modalities to deliver
patient-centred, outcome-optimised care."

✅ JJ voice:
"When you come to see me, I take a full history — not just your symptoms, but what's
happening in your life, your relationships, your work."

❌ Fake reassurance:
"Don't worry, most men your age experience this!"

✅ JJ voice:
"This is more common than you'd think, but common doesn't mean you have to accept it."

❌ Outcome promises:
"Our treatments have helped hundreds of men regain full sexual function."

✅ JJ voice:
"I can't promise a particular outcome — erections are complex. What I can promise is
a thorough investigation and an honest conversation about your options."

## Regulatory Requirements

### PIF Tick (mandatory metadata for every page)
- Author name + credentials
- Date produced / last reviewed
- Date of next review
- Evidence base (guidelines / studies cited)
- Purpose of the information

### YMYL / E-E-A-T signals
- All clinical claims must be supportable
- Distinguish guideline (NICE/BAUS/EAU) from evidence-based opinion from personal practice
- Link to primary sources where possible

### Red flags — NEVER write these
Medical: "cure" (for chronic conditions), "100% success rate", "no risks",
"guaranteed results", invented statistics

Voice: "Don't worry, it's nothing!", "Our world-class team", "revolutionary treatment",
"cutting-edge technology" (unless genuinely novel)

Ethical: hidden complications, overstated benefits, pressure to choose private over NHS,
dismissal of alternative approaches

## Page Types and Their Specific Rules

### Spoke pages (dedicated condition/treatment)
- Logical flow: problem → explanation → investigation → options → next steps
- Contains the conversion CTA (booking or doppler inquiry)
- Microcronica (inline-expand, not full section)
- FAQ section targeting PAA questions from Semrush

### Hub / pillar pages
- Covers head terms semantically
- Routes to spoke pages via linkCardBlock
- Contains myth-busters and interactive self-assessment where appropriate
- IIEF-5 or other calculators if relevant

### Blog posts (Wave 1.5)
- 800–1200 words
- FAQ format
- One primary keyword, conversational
- Links to hub + relevant spoke
- No microcronica needed unless particularly compelling

### Sensitive topic handling
```html
<!-- GOOD: sensitive, normalising -->
<p>Sexual difficulties are incredibly common and nothing to feel embarrassed about.
Roughly half of men experience erectile problems at some point.</p>

<p>In my clinic, these conversations happen every day. You won't shock me, and you're
not wasting my time. This is what I do.</p>

<!-- BAD: dismissive -->
<p>Many men suffer from these issues unnecessarily. Simply come in and we'll fix the
problem quickly.</p>
```

### Psychosexual / mental health intersection
```html
<p>Sexual function and mental health are deeply connected. Performance anxiety,
depression, and relationship stress can all affect erections — and erectile problems
can worsen anxiety and low mood. It's a cycle that's hard to break alone.</p>

<p>While I'm a urologist not a psychiatrist, I work closely with psychosexual therapists
and can refer you for specialist psychological support if that would help.</p>
```
