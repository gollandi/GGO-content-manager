# Style Guide — Legal Page

**Status: STUB (v0.1) — full documentation in v0.2+**

Legal pages are the **compliance and transparency** pages of ggomed.co.uk —
terms, privacy, cookies, accessibility, complaints. They are rarely created
new (most updates go via Daria), but when they are, they have a distinctly
different register from clinical content.

---

## 1. When a page is a legal page

A legal page:

- Is a **compliance / transparency** document (not clinical content)
- Lives at `/legal/[slug]` or similar prefixed path
- Has a distinctly different **register**: formal, precise, legally accurate
- May be **partially generated by a legal template** (with JJ's firm / ICO
  guidance as source) and then adapted to GGOMed's specifics
- Requires **version history discipline** — legal pages carry effective-date
  and revision metadata

**Examples of legal pages:**
- `/legal/terms-of-service`
- `/legal/privacy-policy`
- `/legal/cookie-policy`
- `/legal/accessibility-statement`
- `/legal/complaints-procedure`

---

## 2. Register — where legal diverges from clinical voice

This is the biggest difference vs. any other GGOMed page type:

- **Second-person "you" still applies** (the user, the patient)
- **First-person "I" is replaced by "GGO Med" / "GGO Med Ltd"** — this is the
  business speaking, not JJ the clinician
- **Formal register** — "we use cookies" not "I set cookies"
- **Precise legal / regulatory terminology** — data controller, processor,
  retention period, lawful basis (GDPR), cookie categories (necessary /
  preference / analytics / marketing)
- **Clinical voice rules relax**: duty-of-candour phrasing doesn't apply;
  evidence-tier language doesn't apply — legal precision does
- **Warmth is dialled down** — legal pages are not meant to be "warm". They
  are accurate. A restrained, respectful tone is correct.

---

## 3. Canonical structure (varies by legal doc type)

Legal pages are less structurally uniform than clinical pages. Each legal
doc has its own canonical form:

### Terms of Service (typical)
1. Who we are (entity, address, registration)
2. Scope of terms
3. Services provided
4. Booking and payment
5. Cancellations and refunds
6. Medical information disclaimer
7. Limitation of liability (where lawful)
8. Governing law and jurisdiction
9. Changes to terms
10. Contact details

### Privacy Policy (GDPR)
1. Who we are (data controller details)
2. What personal data we collect
3. Why we collect it (purposes)
4. Lawful basis for processing
5. Who we share it with (processors, third parties)
6. International transfers
7. Retention periods
8. Your rights
9. How to contact us / the ICO
10. Updates to this policy

### Cookie Policy
1. What cookies are
2. Categories of cookies we use (necessary / preference / analytics / marketing)
3. Per-cookie list (name, purpose, duration, first/third party)
4. How to manage cookies (browser + site preferences)
5. Updates to this policy

Each of these should mirror the GGOMed practice reality — not a generic
template. JJ's source document (if provided) is authoritative; the writer's
job is to reshape it into house voice without altering legal meaning.

---

## 4. Best-effort interactive block pattern

Legal pages are **mostly plain prose**. Minimal interactive blocks:

| Block type | Target count | Placement |
|------------|--------------|-----------|
| Accordion (FAQ / section groups) | 0–1 | Only if the legal doc is very long and benefits from collapse |
| Simple card (alert) | 0–1 | Only for genuinely critical notices |
| Link cards | 0–1 | "Contact us" / "ICO" link at end |
| Table | 0–1 | Cookie list table in cookie policy; retention periods in privacy |

Legal pages should NOT use inline-expand, quizzes, feature cards, or
myth-buster accordions. The register doesn't fit.

---

## 5. PIF Tick / regulatory note

- Legal pages are **not PIF Tick eligible** (PIF Tick is for health
  information, not commercial terms)
- Legal pages still carry a "Last updated" and effective-date field
- If a legal page affects clinical pathway (e.g. medical disclaimer terms),
  cross-reference clinical pages where appropriate
